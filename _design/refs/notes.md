# Kouchd niche reference review

**Query:** `shared living management`  
**Market:** US App Store search, fetched 2026-10-19  
**Reference set:** six non-Kouchd results selected by the design-reference tool and shown in `sheet.jpg`.

## What the shelf has in common

- The first frame makes one promise in a large headline, then shows a phone or product surface that proves it.
- Later frames are feature cards: one job per frame, with short benefit-led copy and a visible UI state.
- Screens use strong contrast and simple backgrounds. The dominant patterns are a light canvas with dark type or a saturated color field behind a centered device.
- The sequence moves from orientation to action: overview, a concrete task, a status or record, then another workflow.
- Product chrome is kept legible in the screenshots. Lists, cards, dashboards, profiles, payments, messaging, and controls are used as evidence rather than decorative mockups.

## What repeats across the references

- **LOFT Living:** dark and saturated promotional frames, large benefit copy, a resident home dashboard, announcements, payment, and rewards.
- **SmartLife: Smart Living:** cool blue feature fields and dense control surfaces for devices, scenes, home protection, and energy reporting.
- **TownSq Community:** green and white community utility screens: inbox, profile, maintenance, and project updates.
- **Notion:** editorial black-on-white headlines, illustration-led opening, then workspace, wiki, and travel-planning screens.
- **Westdale Living:** dark navy resident-portal treatment with sign-in, account balance, payment, and notifications.
- **Customer App - Zoho Assist:** green support workflow with technician contact, screen sharing, chat, and pause/resume controls.

Together they make the category look like a resident portal, smart-home controller, community utility, or general productivity workspace. The visual language is usually operational and feature-dense; only Notion uses a strongly editorial opening.

## Kouchd's first-glance difference

Kouchd should lead with the human coordination problem: **one shared stay, clearly held by the people involved**. Its first frame can pair the warm cream / forest / lime system with a calm, plainspoken promise and an unmistakable shared-stay record. The next frames should prove the specific loop that the references do not show together:

1. Set dates, house details, and expectations for a temporary stay.
2. Keep a contribution record that people can confirm or dispute; Kouchd records contributions and does not move money.
3. Coordinate chores, check-ins, and occupied nights.
4. Make the host / guest roles explicit, with guests free to join a stay.

That combination should read as shared living between people, rather than a landlord portal, smart-device dashboard, generic task manager, or payment app. Keep the open space, large promise, and one-job-per-frame structure learned from the shelf, while preserving Kouchd's warmer palette, host-and-guest framing, and visible boundaries around contribution records.

## Guardrail
